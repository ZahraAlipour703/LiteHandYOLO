Abstract

3D Morphable Models (3DMMs) remain the standard parametric shape priors behind state-of-the-art 3D face reconstruction. Because they are built from a finite set of 3D face scans, they inherit the morphological biases of that data, which can limit how well they generalize across diverse populations.

We analyze 3DMM reconstructions through the lens of surface curvature, in order to discover, quantify, and visualize these biases. Where standard metrics rely on Euclidean distances, our error captures subtle surface nuances such as local topology and undulations. Using the Laplace-Beltrami operator, we generate high-resolution curvature error maps that give a localized, geometrically meaningful picture of where a reconstruction departs from the ground truth. We derive a scalar metric from these maps and validate it in a user study, finding a significantly higher correlation with human perception than traditional methods.

Across several 3DMM bases and fitting algorithms, we uncover systematic age-related biases and present preliminary evidence of gender- and ethnicity-related biases. These findings argue for curvature-aware evaluation protocols to ensure both demographic fairness and geometric precision in future 3D face reconstruction research.

Method

To quantify the preservation of structural identity and identify systematic geometric biases, we propose to use the Mean Curvature, a second-order differential property characterizing the local geometry of the manifold. Unlike point-wise Euclidean metrics, mean curvature is sensitive to the local surface “character”—such as ridges, valleys, and high-frequency morphological details—making it a robust indicator of reconstruction fidelity. We propose to calculate mean curvature by locally estimating the Laplace-Beltrami operator of the surface.

What we had before: NMSE

where is point-to-surface mapping.


  • Point-to-surface distance
  • Global fidelity only
  • Insensitive to ridges, creases, folds
  • Near-random alignment with human judgment (accuracy ≈50%)
  • Detects no significant age or ethnicity bias

What we propose: Curvature Reconstruction Error (CRE)

where is mean curvature and is the diagonal mass matrix whose entries correspond to the barycentric area for each vertex .

  • Curvature difference
  • Sensitive to local surface geometry
  • Captures ridges, folds, undulations
  • Aligns with human judgment (accuracy 73.6%)
  • Detects significant age bias and preliminary evidence of ethnicity bias
